## My notes

AI systems excel at pattern recognition, yet they face 10 core technical and operational limitations that require active guardrails. Deploying these systems effectively involves balancing automation benefits against safety and compliance risks across specific real-world use cases.

10 AI Limitations and Mitigations

• Hallucination: Models generate plausible-sounding but factually false or fabricated information. Mitigation: Ground responses in verified external knowledge bases using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and enforce strict citation checks.
• Algorithmic Bias: Models replicate or amplify historical prejudices present in their training data. Mitigation: Audit datasets for demographic balance and apply fairness constraints during model training and post-processing.
• Data and Concept Drift: Model accuracy degrades over time as real-world input data or underlying relationships change. Mitigation: Implement continuous automated monitoring to detect statistical shifts and trigger scheduled model retraining.
• Overfitting: Models memorize noise and specific quirks in training data instead of learning generalizable patterns. Mitigation: Apply regularization techniques (like dropout or weight decay) and evaluate performance on held-out validation sets.
• Lack of Explainability: Complex deep neural networks operate as "black boxes," hiding how specific inputs lead to outputs. Mitigation: Use interpretability frameworks like SHAP or LIME to generate feature-importance scores for individual predictions.
• Adversarial Vulnerability: Maliciously crafted inputs (such as prompt injections or subtle image perturbations) trick models into failing. Mitigation: Deploy input/output sanitization filters and train models on adversarial examples to build robustness.
• Context Window Constraints: Models lose track of instructions or forget earlier details when processing long documents or extended conversations. Mitigation: Use semantic chunking, sliding context windows, or external vector memory to retrieve only the most relevant snippets.
• Data Privacy Leakage: Models can inadvertently memorize and regurgitate sensitive personally identifiable information (PII) from training sets. Mitigation: Scrub PII prior to training and apply differential privacy techniques to inject mathematical noise into gradients.
• Lack of Causal Reasoning: AI excels at spotting statistical correlations but fails to understand true cause-and-effect relationships. Mitigation: Combine machine learning with domain-specific business rules or causal inference graphs for high-stakes decisions.
• High Resource Consumption: Training and running large-scale models demand massive compute power, energy, and financial cost. Mitigation: Compress models using quantization, pruning, or knowledge distillation to run efficiently on smaller hardware.
